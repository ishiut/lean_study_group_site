## Why Lean? 

[Lean](https://lean-lang.org/) is an open-source programming language and proof assistant designed to verify algorithm correctness and construct formal (machine-verifiable) mathematical proofs.

First launched in 2013, Lean has experienced rapid adoption among mathematicians in recent years. 
Prominent mathematicians, including Terence Tao and Tim Gowers, are actively involved in this project. 

Recently, there has been a surge of reports of AI systems solving open problems in mathematics. 
Because hallucination is still an issue, proof assistants like Lean are used to provide verification. 
Namely, AI and proof assistants complement each other in the use of computers for mathematical research. 

In 2026 alone, major AI-assisted breakthroughs and formalization milestones include: 

- A counterexample to Jacobian Conjecture
- A counterexample to Erdős Unit Distance Conjecture
- Raising the lower bound of non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line to 67.2% (up from 41.6%)
- Formalization of the known proof of Fermat's Last Theorem. 

While AI can write longer and more complicated formal proofs, we, human mathematicians, may want to inspect, adjust, and correct them to understand their arguments. 
This study group aims to give you the practical skills to write and read formal proofs yourself.
